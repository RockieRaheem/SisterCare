# Partnership Proposal

## Transforming Sheebah Cares into an always-on digital care platform for women and girls

**Proposed by:** Kamwanga Raheem, Founder, SisterCare  
**Proposed to:** Sheebah Karungi and the Sheebah Cares leadership team  
**Proposed brand:** **Sheebah Cares — powered by SisterCare**  
**Date:** 30 July 2026  
**Status:** Confidential discussion draft

---

## Executive summary

Sheebah Karungi has used her voice, lived experience and public influence to
advance menstrual dignity and support women and girls. The launch of Holic Pads
and subsequent menstrual-health campaigns demonstrated the potential of
combining a trusted public figure, accessible products, education and community
partnerships.

SisterCare proposes the next stage of that mission: an always-available digital
care platform through which women and girls can privately understand their
cycles, receive pregnancy support, access clinically reviewed information,
communicate in familiar languages and connect with verified human counsellors
when they need more than digital guidance.

Under the proposed partnership, SisterCare would be adapted and introduced as
**Sheebah Cares — powered by SisterCare**. Following a successful pilot and
completion of the required brand, legal, clinical and operational agreements,
the parties could transition the platform to the full **Sheebah Cares** identity.

This is more than a name change. It is an opportunity to transform Sheebah
Cares from campaign-based engagement into a measurable, trusted and scalable
care ecosystem that remains available between campaigns, product distributions
and public appearances.

## The opportunity

Menstrual-health interventions are most useful when they combine safe products,
accurate information, privacy, supportive adults and access to appropriate
health services. Uganda continues to face menstrual stigma, product-access
barriers and school disruption. UNFPA has reported that girls in surveyed
Ugandan districts missed school during menstruation, while Uganda has since
introduced national Menstrual Health Guidelines for Schools and Education
Institutions.

Sheebah Cares is well positioned to respond because it can bring together:

- Sheebah's trusted public voice and connection with young women;
- Holic Pads' experience in menstrual-product access and awareness;
- SisterCare's private digital health-support technology;
- verified counsellors and clinically governed education;
- schools, community organisations, health facilities and development partners;
- measurable, privacy-safe evidence of reach and outcomes.

Today, a pad-distribution or awareness campaign may reach a participant once.
The proposed platform creates a continuing relationship: before her next
period, during symptoms, when she has a pregnancy concern, when she needs
reliable information, or when she wants to speak with a trained person.

## The proposed solution

SisterCare is a mobile-first health-support platform designed around three
distinct user groups.

### For members

Women and girls can:

- privately track menstrual-cycle and pregnancy information;
- record symptoms, preferences and important health updates;
- receive timely reminders and personalised educational guidance;
- have persistent conversations in English or Luganda;
- access clinically reviewed articles;
- request support from an eligible, available counsellor;
- retain control over their account and personal information.

### For counsellors

Approved professionals receive a dedicated workspace where they can:

- complete a private KYC and credential-verification process;
- control whether they are available to receive sessions;
- receive assignments only when verified, online, on shift and within capacity;
- manage care sessions through a defined lifecycle;
- contribute professional articles for editorial review.

### For administrators

Authorised Sheebah Cares operators receive a separate administration portal
where they can:

- review counsellor identity and professional credentials;
- manage counsellor capacity, shifts and safety eligibility;
- approve clinically appropriate library content;
- monitor urgent-care handoff times and incidents;
- review privacy-safe service and impact indicators;
- maintain an audit trail for consequential operational decisions.

### Responsible digital assistance

The platform includes a conversational support assistant that can use the
member's permitted profile and conversation history to provide more continuous
support. It can perform only defined, authorised platform actions. Safety
triage, human escalation and consequential access controls remain governed by
deterministic rules and accountable people rather than being left solely to a
generative model.

The assistant is not presented as a doctor and does not replace diagnosis,
antenatal care, emergency services or professional counselling.

## Why this benefits Sheebah Cares

### 1. It turns public influence into continuous care

Sheebah's audience can move from hearing a campaign message to using a
practical service throughout the month. This creates sustained value beyond a
concert, social post, school visit or product donation.

### 2. It establishes a distinctive and credible digital product

Rather than launching another general awareness campaign, Sheebah Cares would
offer a functioning care platform with member, counsellor and administration
experiences. This creates a more defensible identity in women's health and
social impact.

### 3. It strengthens the Holic Pads mission

Holic Pads can remain a product brand while Sheebah Cares becomes the wider
support ecosystem around menstrual dignity. Product education, period
preparedness and community distribution campaigns could be connected without
making product purchase a condition for receiving care.

### 4. It creates measurable impact

With appropriate consent and privacy protection, the organisation can report
aggregated measures such as:

- members reached and meaningfully activated;
- educational content completed;
- changes in menstrual-health knowledge;
- counsellor requests and time to human response;
- successful referrals;
- campaign reach by district or partner cohort;
- service reliability and safety-escalation performance.

No sponsor or public report should receive identifiable cycle, pregnancy,
conversation or counselling data.

### 5. It increases partnership and funding potential

A governed platform with documented workflows and measurable outcomes creates
a stronger proposition for schools, health providers, telecommunications
companies, development agencies, foundations and responsible corporate
sponsors.

### 6. It supports responsible regional growth

The system can progressively support additional languages, country-specific
content, local referral pathways and counsellor networks. Expansion would occur
only after local clinical, legal and safeguarding review.

### 7. It protects and extends Sheebah's legacy

The platform converts advocacy into lasting infrastructure. Women and girls can
receive support even when Sheebah is not personally present, while her mission
and voice remain central to the experience.

## Benefits to women and girls

The partnership would give users:

- a private and non-judgmental entry point to menstrual and reproductive-health
  information;
- continuity, so they do not have to repeat their story in every interaction;
- locally understandable language and culturally appropriate communication;
- a clear path from general education to qualified human help;
- greater preparation for periods and important pregnancy milestones;
- access that is not dependent on attending a physical campaign;
- transparent controls over personal information.

The platform should remain inclusive of adult women, adolescents, pregnant
people and users with different levels of literacy, connectivity and
disability. It should never use language that infantilises adult women or makes
assumptions about marital status, pregnancy intentions or sexual activity.

## Brand and rebranding proposal

### Recommended first-stage identity

**Sheebah Cares**  
*Powered by SisterCare*

This gives the public one clear care brand while preserving transparency about
the technology during the pilot. The interface would adopt an approved Sheebah
Cares visual system, voice, domain and campaign language without copying
Sheebah's personal identity in ways she has not expressly authorised.

### Transition to the full Sheebah Cares name

A full rebrand should occur after:

1. Sheebah Cares confirms its exact legal entity and authorised signatory;
2. the parties sign trademark, brand-licensing and technology agreements;
3. ownership of the software, improvements, content and user relationships is
   documented;
4. privacy notices identify the correct data controller and processors;
5. clinical and safeguarding owners are formally appointed;
6. existing SisterCare users are clearly informed and, where legally required,
   provide consent before any material change;
7. domains, application-store identities and official support channels are
   secured;
8. the pilot meets agreed safety, service and impact thresholds.

The partnership may then choose one of three long-term structures:

- **Co-branded partnership:** Sheebah Cares remains the public brand and
  SisterCare remains the disclosed technology partner.
- **Licensed white-label platform:** Sheebah Cares licenses and operates a
  branded version under defined service and governance agreements.
- **Strategic acquisition or joint venture:** ownership, investment and
  governance are negotiated separately after product and market validation.

The recommended starting model is the co-branded partnership because it is
fastest to test and clearest about accountability.

## Proposed 90-day pilot

### Phase 1: Partnership and safety foundation — weeks 1 to 3

- confirm legal entities, decision-makers and brand authority;
- agree target population, locations and partner organisations;
- appoint clinical, safeguarding and data-protection leads;
- review all health content, Luganda communication and emergency pathways;
- approve privacy, consent, child-safeguarding and incident-response processes;
- define pilot indicators and baseline research;
- complete the Sheebah Cares visual and content adaptation.

### Phase 2: Controlled launch — weeks 4 to 8

- onboard a limited, clearly defined member cohort;
- verify and train the initial counsellor team;
- launch education, cycle support and human-referral services;
- connect selected community or Holic Pads activities to voluntary platform
  onboarding;
- provide monitored support and rapid incident resolution;
- collect structured user feedback without exposing private health content.

### Phase 3: Evaluation and scale decision — weeks 9 to 12

- evaluate safety, reliability, usability and member trust;
- assess counsellor response performance and operational capacity;
- compare knowledge and preparedness measures with the pilot baseline;
- document improvements required before public expansion;
- publish an agreed, privacy-safe pilot report;
- decide whether to scale, revise, pause or enter a full rebranding agreement.

## How success should be measured

The pilot should agree targets before launch. Its scorecard should include:

- successful registration and onboarding rate;
- 7-day and 30-day meaningful retention;
- member-reported usefulness and trust;
- improvement in tested menstrual-health knowledge;
- response completeness in supported languages;
- median and 90th-percentile time to human support;
- percentage of urgent cases receiving the required escalation;
- counsellor acceptance, completion and quality indicators;
- platform uptime and failed-request rate;
- privacy, safeguarding and clinical incidents;
- percentage of users who understand that the assistant is not a doctor.

Downloads, impressions and celebrity reach may be reported, but they should not
be treated as proof of improved health outcomes.

## Responsibilities

### SisterCare would provide

- the core platform and technical adaptation;
- member, counsellor and administration portals;
- system security, access controls and operational auditability;
- platform training and technical documentation;
- incident investigation and reliability support;
- implementation of approved branding and content;
- privacy-safe measurement infrastructure.

### Sheebah Cares would provide

- written brand authorisation and approved public messaging;
- an accountable partnership and operations lead;
- access to clinical, safeguarding and community-program expertise;
- counsellor recruitment or approved care partners;
- campaign reach and stakeholder introductions;
- review of cultural tone and community appropriateness;
- agreed pilot operating resources.

### Joint responsibilities

- clinical governance and content approval;
- user safeguarding and escalation pathways;
- data-protection compliance;
- partner and counsellor due diligence;
- pilot design, evaluation and public reporting;
- crisis communication and reputation management.

## Commercial and sustainability framework

The final budget should be developed after a joint discovery workshop and
should separate:

- one-time branding and implementation;
- platform hosting and technical operations;
- clinical and safeguarding review;
- counsellor staffing and supervision;
- user support;
- community activation and product distribution;
- monitoring, research and evaluation;
- future language or country expansion.

Possible sustainable funding can include institutional sponsorship,
development-partner grants, responsibly structured employer or school
programs, funded counselling capacity and premium convenience features for
adults who can pay. Essential safety information and emergency guidance should
never be placed behind a payment barrier.

Commercial sponsorship must not influence clinical answers or allow sponsors to
target users using private reproductive-health information.

## Essential safeguards

Before the platform is offered to real users, the partnership should require:

- named clinical and safeguarding accountability;
- verified counsellors with current credentials;
- clear limits on what digital guidance can do;
- age-appropriate consent and child-protection procedures;
- country-specific emergency and referral information;
- encryption, role-based access and privacy-by-design;
- a defined retention and account-deletion policy;
- independent security and clinical testing;
- accessible complaints and support channels;
- transparent incident review and corrective action.

## Proposed decision and next step

We request a 60-minute partnership meeting with Sheebah Karungi and the
authorised Sheebah Cares representatives to:

1. demonstrate the working SisterCare platform;
2. confirm Sheebah Cares' priorities and legal structure;
3. select the partnership and brand model;
4. agree the pilot population and responsible clinical partners;
5. nominate one decision-maker from each party;
6. authorise a two-week joint discovery and pilot-planning exercise.

Following that meeting, SisterCare would produce a final implementation scope,
budget, governance matrix, risk register, brand concept and 90-day pilot
agreement for approval.

## Closing

Sheebah has already helped bring menstrual dignity into public conversation.
SisterCare can provide the digital infrastructure needed to make that support
private, continuous, measurable and connected to accountable human care.

Together, we can build Sheebah Cares into more than a campaign or application:
a trusted care network that listens to women, respects their privacy and helps
them move from uncertainty to informed action.
